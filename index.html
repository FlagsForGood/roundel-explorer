About This Database

This interactive tool accompanies the paper From Flags to Flight: The Vexillological Evolution of Aircraft Identification from Maritime Law to Modern Livery, presented at ICV31 — the 31st International Congress of Vexillology.

The Three Translation Methods

Every roundel is classified by how it translates its national flag into a circular aircraft marking. The taxonomy reduces to three primary methods, each with variants — explained in full on the page.

Radial
The flag's stripes are wrapped into concentric rings around a center. Horizontal tribands rotate around the bottom edge; vertical tribands around the hoist. Variants cover inversion, segmentation, preserved or internalized hoist elements, and added charges. France (1912) established the template.
Punch
A self-contained charge is extracted — "punched" — from the flag and set directly in the disc, carrying the flag's meaning without needing its field. Japan's hinomaru is the purest case.
Non Sequitur
The roundel does not follow from the current flag: an inherited template (RAF-style tricolors kept across the Commonwealth), an abandoned older design, or a symbol from military heraldry. Not a failure — an acknowledgement that some flags have no clean circular translation.
